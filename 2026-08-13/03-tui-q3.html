Stranded Cruise Ships, Higher Fuel Prices: Iran War Eats Into TUI’s Profits

Bailey Schulz
August 12th, 2026 at 1:04 PM EDT

Skift Take

Bookings were softer in the company’s third quarter, but executives said business has started to normalize in recent weeks.

German travel company TUI Group reported a dip in profitability in its latest quarter as the company struggled to shake pressures from the Iran War.

Key Points

  • Quarterly EBIT fell 27% to €233.8 million, with the Iran War costing roughly €20 million in the quarter and an estimated €60 million over nine months due to stranded cruise ships, repatriation costs, higher fuel, and softer bookings.
  • Booking momentum is recovering—booked revenue rose 7% year-over-year in the past four weeks—and TUI reaffirmed full-year EBIT guidance of €1.1–1.4 billion, with the CEO confident of exceeding the low end.
  • Segment results were mixed: Markets + Airline swung to a €17.4 million loss and Cruises and Hotels each dipped, while TUI Musement (tours and activities) grew EBIT 9.4% on stronger B2B sales; management admitted it may have over-discounted flights to stimulate demand that rebounded anyway.

Summary

TUI Group reported a 27% drop in quarterly EBIT to €233.8 million (~$270 million), with the Iran War weighing heavily on results—costing roughly €20 million directly in the quarter and an estimated €60 million across the first nine months of the fiscal year. The conflict stranded cruise ships in Middle Eastern ports, drove costly repatriations of guests and crew, raised fuel prices, and dampened bookings, particularly in the Markets + Airline segment, which swung to a loss of €17.4 million. Despite the setback, management struck an optimistic tone: booked revenue over the past four weeks rose 7% year-over-year, and TUI reaffirmed full-year underlying EBIT guidance of €1.1 billion to €1.4 billion, with CEO Sebastian Ebel signaling confidence in landing above the low end. Notably, Ebel conceded the company may have cut flight prices too aggressively between March and June to stimulate demand that ultimately rebounded on its own, offering a cautionary lesson for operators exposed to Eastern Mediterranean and Gulf-adjacent routes.
